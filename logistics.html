Logistics

How the course works

This course combines conceptual understanding with sustained practical work. Every week, we study the ideas together and then use them to build, test, and discuss real systems.

Weekly rhythm

We meet for two 90-minute sessions each week. The two sessions have different but complementary roles.

Session 01 · 1h30

Lecture

  • Concepts and mathematical foundations
  • Visual explanations and demonstrations
  • Guided examples
  • Discussion of common misconceptions

Session 02 · 1h30

Project Studio

  • Programming exercises and experiments
  • Weekly project milestones
  • Debugging and instructor feedback
  • Occasional peer review and presentations

Students should arrive at the Project Studio having reviewed the week’s notes and prepared the required code and development environment. The studio is working time: participation, experimentation, and questions are expected.

Communication and discussion

ED

ED Discussion

ED Discussion will be our main communication space for questions, announcements, technical problems, useful resources, and conversations that continue beyond the classroom.

Open ED Discussion →

Students are encouraged to answer questions as well as ask them. Explaining an idea clearly is an important part of learning it, and a useful discussion can help the whole class move forward.

ImportantDiscussion contributes 10% of the course grade

ED activity forms the Discussion and course engagement half of the 20% engagement grade. Assessment rewards sustained, useful participation across the semester—not the largest number of posts.

A useful weekly rhythm

Aim to make at least one substantive contribution during most course weeks. This is a rhythm rather than a message quota: one precise question, careful explanation, or useful experiment is more valuable than several superficial replies.

Before posting a question, show what you tried and where your reasoning became uncertain. When answering, help the other student take the next step without completing assessed work for them. Return to your discussions when possible to confirm what resolved the issue or summarize what the class learned.

Meaningful participation can include:

  • Asking a precise question after making an initial attempt
  • Giving a clear and constructive explanation
  • Sharing a useful experiment, visualization, or reference
  • Identifying and correcting an error
  • Summarizing the conclusion of a discussion
  • Helping a classmate debug without posting a complete solution

ED participation is assessed by usefulness, regularity, and intellectual contribution—not by message count. Short, superficial, or repetitive posts do not improve the participation grade.

Weekly projects and homework · 40%

Practical work is distributed across the semester. Most projects develop over several weeks and follow the thematic parts shown on the course schedule.

This component evaluates:

  • Correct and thoughtful implementation
  • Experimental method and interpretation
  • Understanding of the underlying ideas
  • Clear communication of results
  • Steady progress across project milestones

Engagement and participation · 20%

10%

Attendance and studio participation

Regular presence, preparation, active project work, and constructive collaboration.

10%

Discussion and course engagement

Meaningful ED contributions, questions, explanations, and shared learning.

Participation is about consistent engagement, not simply occupying a seat or accumulating posts. The detailed attendance and justified-absence policy will be announced before classes begin.

Final assessment · 40%

Format to be confirmed: this component will be either a substantial group project or a final examination. The final format and evaluation rubric will be announced before the course begins.

If a group project is selected, assessment will include both the quality of the group’s work and each student’s individual understanding and contribution.
